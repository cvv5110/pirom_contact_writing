\section{Modeling of Hypersonic Thermal Protection Systems}

The following discussion presents the modeling of the transient heat conduction problem in multi-layered hypersonic thermal protection systems (TPS) with imperfect interfaces applications. Specifically, three different approaches of decreasing fidelity are presented for solving the governing equations: (i) a high-resolution three-dimensional FEM termed the \emph{full-order model} (FOM), (ii) a coarse one-dimensional FEM model with linear elements termed the \emph{one-dimensional FEM} (1D-FEM), and (iii) a finite-volume-based lumped capacitance model (LCM) with interfacial temperature jump dynamics termed the \emph{reduced-physics model} (RPM). The three solution strategies are described in detail next.

\subsection{Governing Equations}

Consider the generic domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$ show in Fig.~\ref{fig_general_domain}. A heat flux $q_b$ (i.e., Neumann boundary condition) is prescribed on boundary $e_q$, while a temperature $T_b$ (i.e., Dirichlet boundary condition) is prescribed on boundary $e_T$, and $\partial\Omega = \cup_{i=1}^{N}\partial\Omega_i$ where $e_{iq},e_{iT}\subset\partial\Omega_i$. The transient heat transfer is governed by the following energy PDE,
\begin{subequations}
 \begin{align}
    \rho c_p \partial_t T - \nabla\cdot\left(\vk(T)\nabla T\right) &= 0, \: x\in\Omega\\
    -\vk(T)\nabla T\cdot\vn &= q_b(r,t), \: r\in e_q\\
    T(r,t) &= T_b(r,t), \: r\in e_T\\
    T(r,0) &= T_0(r), \: x\in\Omega
 \end{align}
 \label{eq:gen_eq}
\end{subequations}
\noindent where the density $\rho$ is constant, while the heat capacity $c_p$ and thermal conductivity $\vk\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ are temperature-dependent. The TPS domain is further divided into $N$ non-overlapping components $\left\{\Omega_i\right\}_{i=1}^{N}$, with $\NGamma$ different imperfect interfaces between components. Specifically, to facilitate subsequent model derivations, each domain $\Omega_i$ is further subdivided into: (i) a \emph{contact subdomain} $\Omega_i^c$ consisting of the portion of TPS layer that is in direct contact with a neighboring layer across and imperfect interface, and (ii) a \emph{bulk subdomain} $\Omega_i^b$ consisting of the remaining interior bulk material; clearly, $\Omega_i = \Omega_i^b\cup\Omega_i^c$, and $\Omega_i^b\cap\Omega_i^c = \emptyset$.

For example, Fig.~\ref{fig_general_domain} contains $N_C=3$ components and $N_{\Gamma}=3$ imperfect contacts between components. Such partition of the TPS is introduced to systematically model the \emph{bulk} and \emph{contact} heat transfer as separate but coupled mechanisms, which becomes critical in subsequent sections to infer TCRs and learn the hidden dynamics in the PIROM. The following discussion introduces three different approaches of decreasing fidelity to modeling the hypersonic TPS system: (i) a FOM based on a three-dimensional FEM, (ii) a coarse one-dimensional FEM, and (iii) a coarse finite-volume, i.e., lumped capacitance model (LCM).

\subsection{Full-Order Finite-Element Model}

The FOM is obtained by spatially discretizing the governing PDE using a Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) FEM, to result in a high-dimensional system of ODEs that model the transient temperature dynamics of the TPS. The DG-FEM formulation is leveraged for discretization as it: (i) provides a systematic framework for modeling multi-layered TPS with complex geometries, varying material properties, and interfacial TCRs, and (ii) is amenable to systematic model-order reduction via coarse-graining. Note that the high-fidelity numerical simulations in Sec. \hl{[X]} are performed using standard FEM instead, and the equivalence between DG and standard FEM is noted upon their convergence.

Consider a conforming mesh partition of the TPS domain $\Omega$, were each element belongs to one and only one of the components $\left\{\Omega_i\right\}_{i=1}^{N}$, and where each domain $\Omega_i$ is further divided into \emph{bulk} and \emph{contact} sub-domains, i.e., $\Omega_i=\Omega_i^b\cup\Omega_i^c$. Denote the collection of elements on the bulk sub-domains as $\left\{B_i\right\}_{i=1}^{B}$, and on the contact sub-domains as $\left\{W_i\right\}_{i=1}^{K}$, so that $\left\{E_i\right\}_{i=1}^{M}$ with $M=K+B$ corresponds to the complete collection of elements over the entire TPS. Moreover, the perfect, imperfect, Neumann, and Dirichlet boundary interfaces are denoted as $e_{ij}$, $\Gamma_{ij}$, $e_{iq}$, and $e_{iT}$, respectively. Lastly, for an element $E_i$, $\left|\partial E_i\right|$ denotes the length $(d=2)$ or area $(d=3)$ of a boundary $\partial E_i$. 

On the $i$-th element, use a set of $P$ basis functions, such as polynomials, to represent the temperature field as,
\begin{equation}
    T_i(r,t) = \sum_{p=1}^P \vphi_p(r) x_{i,p}(t),\qquad r\in E_i\label{eqn_temperature_approximation}
\end{equation}
where $\left\{x_{i,p}\right\}_{p=1}^{P}$ corresponds to the states of element $E_i$. Without loss of generality, let the basis functions on element $E_i$ satisfy the orthogonality condition $\int_{E_i}\phi_p^i\phi_q^i d\Omega = \left|E_i\right|\delta_{pq},\: p\neq q$, where $\delta_{pq}$ is the Kronecker delta. Furthermore, to aid in subsequent model-order reduction derivations, choose $\phi_1^i=1$; therefore, by orthogonality, $\int_{E_i}\phi_p^i d\Omega = 0$ for $p>1$. The first basis function therefore captures the volume-averaged temperature of the element, while the remaining basis functions capture the spatially varying temperature distributions within the element.

Using standard variational procedures, e.g., [Pernet2018] as detailed in Appendix [X], the full governing equation is denoted as,
\begin{equation}
    \vcA(\vx)\dot{\vx} = \vcB\left(\vx\right)\vx + \vcF(t)\label{eqn_dg_fem}
\end{equation}
where $\vx=\left[\vu,\vw\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{MP}$ with $M=B+K$ includes the states for $B$ bulk and $K$ contact elements, i.e., $\vcA\in\mathbb{R}^{MP\times MP}$ and $\vcB\in\mathbb{R}^{MP\times MP}$ are the temperature-dependent mass and stiffness matrices, and $\vcF(t)$ corresponds to the contributions from the boundary conditions, respectively.

\subsection{One-Dimensional Finite-Element Model}

A \emph{one-dimensional} FEM model is introduced as a reduced-order representation of the through-thickness thermal response of the TPS. The model employs a coarse mesh in which each material layer is represented by a single finite element with linear trial functions. This approximation is motivated by the predominantly through-thickness heat transfer and the comparatively small in-plane temperature gradients commonly encountered in layered TPS configurations [Gao2026, X].

Consider a TPS composed of $N$ non-overlapping elements. The temperature within the $i$-th element is approximated as in \cref{eqn_temperature_approximation} with $P=2$. For instance, in the $i$-th element, $\boldsymbol{\phi}_i(x) = \left[\phi_{i,1}(x),\phi_{i,2}(x)\right]^\top$ contains linear trial functions, and $\boldsymbol{u}_i(t) = \left[u_{i,1}(t),u_{i,2}(t)\right]^\top$ contains the corresponding left and right nodal temperature states. Collecting the states for all layers into a single vector yields $\boldsymbol{u} = \left[\boldsymbol{u}_1^\top,\boldsymbol{u}_2^\top,\dots,\boldsymbol{u}_N^\top\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}$. The layer-wise degrees of freedom are retained independently so that temperature discontinuities may be captured at imperfect contacts.

Using the standard Galerkin procedure, the governing equations are written in matrix form as,
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}})
    \dot{\boldsymbol{u}}
    =
    \left[
        \boldsymbol{B}^{b}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}})
        +
        \boldsymbol{B}^{c}
    \right]\boldsymbol{u}
    +
    \boldsymbol{f}(t),
    \label{eq:1d_fem}
\end{equation}
where \(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}=[\bar{u}_1,\ldots,\bar{u}_N]^{\top}\) contains the layer-average temperatures, \(\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times2N}\) is the heat-capacity matrix, \(\boldsymbol{B}^{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times2N}\) is the bulk-conduction matrix, \(\boldsymbol{B}^{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times2N}\)  accounts for imperfect thermal contacts, and \(\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}\) contains the contributions from the prescribed boundary conditions.

--------------

\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}})\dot{\boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{B}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{F}(t)
    \label{eq:full_mat_form}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}$ are block diagonals containing the mass and stiffness matrices for each element, and $\boldsymbol{F}$
is the forcing vector upholding the boundary condition at each edge. In a TPS system with perfect contacts, these components are solely products of the choice in basis function; for LBFs, they are defined as
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:perf_cont}
    \begin{align}
        \boldsymbol{A}_i(\bar{u}) &= \rho c_p(\bar{u})\frac L6 \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \\ \end{bmatrix} 
        \\
        \boldsymbol{B}_i(\bar{u}) &= \frac{\text{k}(\bar{u})}{L} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \\
        \boldsymbol{F} &= \int_{\partial\Omega} \boldsymbol{\phi} q_b \, d\Gamma
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\boldsymbol{A}_i, \boldsymbol{B}_i$ are the $i$-th blocks of $\boldsymbol{A}, \boldsymbol{B}$ respectively, and $\boldsymbol{F}$ is defined for the entire domain via its vector of LBFs, $\boldsymbol{\phi}$. 

For a TPS with imperfect contacts, the bulk stiffness matrix in \cref{eqn:perf_cont} no longer captures the total governing conductance of the TPS system, and thus must also account for the thermal resistances introduced by the imperfect contacts through the following heat interface heat flux condition,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{B}= \underbrace{\boldsymbol{B}^b}_{\text{Satisfied}} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{B}^c}_{\text{Missing}}
\end{equation}
where the superscripts $b$ and $c$ differentiate the stiffness matrices that capture the \emph{bulk} and \emph{contact} heat fluxes, respectively. The choice in modeling the contact stiffness arises from the heat flux law 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:heat_flux_law}
    q = \frac{T_l - T_r}{R_{x}}
\end{equation}
where the flux across a given element is defined by the change in temperature between the left and right sides over its thermal resistance. Leveraging the relationship of conductance and resistance, or 
\begin{equation}
    C = \frac{1}{R_x}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:heat_flux_law} can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
        q = C\left({T^- - T^+}\right)
\end{equation}
where $T^-$ and $T^+$ represent the left and right sides of a given material block, respectively. 
Finally, generalizing for the full set of interactions between two locations on either side of a contact, the contact stiffness becomes
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{B}^c_j = C_{j}\begin{bmatrix}
        1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
for the $j$-th contact, where $j = i-1$ due to the linearity in alignment of the elements (due to the one-dimensional assumption). The contact matrix can also be organized into a block diagonal matrix of shape $\boldsymbol{B}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{2M\times2M}$, whose general structure is
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{B}^c = \begin{bmatrix}
        0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & \boldsymbol{B}_1^c & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
        \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
        0 & 0& \cdots &\boldsymbol{B}^c_j&0\\0&0&\cdots&0&0
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
as there is no contact interface on the edges of the TPS. 

Expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:full_mat_form} to now encompass all aspects of the distant contact problem yields
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{A}(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}})\dot{\boldsymbol{u}} = \boldsymbol{B}^b(\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}){\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{B}^c{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{F}(t)
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{B}^c$ is not a function of average temperature or time, as conductance of an imperfect contact is assumed non-zero and constant. 

\subsection{Reduced-Physics Model}

Next, the \emph{lumped capacitance model} (LCM) under imperfect TPS layer contacts is presented as the RPM for the TPS. In this formulation, each layer is modeled as a lumped mass with a time-varying average temperature, where TCRs introduce additional physics such as intra-layer temperature discontinuities that must be modeled. To handle both bulk and contact heat transfer, the following formulation introduces the decomposition of the TPS layers into \emph{bulk} and \emph{contact} sub-domains, serving as the basis for subsequent coarse-graining derivations, TCR identifiability, and PIROM formulations.

\paragraph*{Bulk Temperature Dynamics} Leveraging our previous work in Ref. [VargasVenegas2026], the \emph{bulk} temperature dynamics of the LCM is derived at the component level from a point of view of energy balance,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \int_{\Omega^{b}_i}\rho c_{p,i}(\bar{u})\dot{\bu}_i d\Omega &= \sum_{j\in\cN^b_i}\int_{e_{ij}}q^{b}_{ij}d\Gamma + \int_{e_{iq}}q_{b}d\Gamma + \int_{e_{iT}}q_{T}d\Gamma\\
        \bar{A}_i\dot{\bar{u}}_i &= \sum_{j\in\cN^b_i}\int_{e_{ij}}q^{b}_{ij}d\Gamma + \int_{e_{iq}}q_{b}d\Gamma + \int_{e_{iT}}q_{T}d\Gamma,\: i=1,2,\dots,N_B\label{eqn_general_lcm}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\bar{A}_i(\bu_i) = \int_{\Omega^{b}_i}\rho c_{p,i}(\bar{u})d\Omega$, and $q^{b}_{ij}$, $q^{c}_{ij}$, $q_{ib}$, and $q_{iT}$ correspond to the heat flux contributions due to neighboring elements with perfect ($e_{ij}$) contact, imperfect ($\Gamma_{ij}$) contact, Neumann boundary conditions ($e_{iq}$), and Dirichlet boundary conditions ($e_{iT}$), respectively. Moreover, $\cN_i^b$ and $\cN_i^c$ are the sets of neighboring elements across perfect and imperfect interfaces, respectively.

Two equivalent approaches are presented in this work to formulate the temperature jump dynamics: (i) coarse-graining the DG-FEM model and applying a \emph{zero-energy storage assumption} for the contact sub-domains as shown in [APPENDIX], and (ii) leveraging energy conservation at the interface level. For brevity, the second approach is adopted here, while the first approach is demonstrated in the [APPENDIX]. By energy conservation, \emph{the bulk and contact heat fluxes are equal} at the imperfect interfaces, leading to the following algebraic constraint,
\begin{equation}
    q^{b}_r(t) = \frac{\bu^{-}-\bu^{+}}{S_r(\bvu) + R_{r}} = \frac{\left(\bu^{-} - \bu^{+}\right) - \Delta\bw_r}{S_r(\bvu)} = g_{r}\Delta\bw_r = q^{c}_r(t)\label{eqn_interface_heat_flux_conservation}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\bw_r$ for $r=1,2,\dots,N_{\Gamma}$ are contact temperature jumps, and depend on the TCR values. The heat flux continuity algebraic constraints in \cref{eqn_interface_heat_flux_conservation} are differentiated with respect to time, i.e., DAE index reduction, such that for each interface,
\begin{equation}
    \left(1 + g_r S_r(\bvu)\right)\Delta\dot{\bw}_r = \bigl(\dot{\bu}^{-} - \dot{\bu}^{+}\bigr) - \frac{\left(\bu^{-} - \bu^{+}\right) - \Delta\bw_r}{S_r(\bvu)}\dot{S}_r(\bvu),\quad r=1,2,\dots,N_{\Gamma}\label{eqn_temperature_jump_dynamics_r}
\end{equation}
Collecting equations from all imperfect interfaces, the following system of ODEs governs the average contact temperature jump,
\begin{equation}
    \left[\vI + \vG\odot\vS(\bvu)\right]\Delta\dot{\bar{\vw}} = \vD\dot{\bvu} - \vG\left(\Delta\bar{\vw}\odot\dot{\vS}(\bvu)\right)
\end{equation}
where the state $\Delta\bar{\vw}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\Gamma}}$, conductance $\vG\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\Gamma}\times N_{\Gamma}}$ and bulk resistance $\vS(\bvu)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\Gamma}\times N_{\Gamma}}$ are defined as,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \vG =& \text{diag}(g_1, g_2, \dots, g_{N_{\Gamma}})\\
        \vS(\bvu) =& \text{diag}(S_1(\bvu), S_2(\bvu), \dots, S_{N_{\Gamma}}(\bvu))
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
while $\vD\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\Gamma}\times N_C}$ is the difference matrix that computes the lumped temperature differences across the interfaces, and $\odot$ denotes the Hadamard product. 

\paragraph*{Reduced-Physics Model Formulation} At component $i$, the following assumptions are employed: (i) the bulk $\bu_i$ and contact $\bw_i$ average temperatures are uniform and equal to the spatial average over the their respective $\Omega_i^b$ and $\Omega_i^{c}$ sub-domains in component $\Omega_i$, (ii) for neighboring components $i$ and $j$ under perfect contact, the bulk heat flux is defined as $q_{ij}^{b} = g_{ij}\left(\bvu\right)\left(\bu_i - \bu_j\right)$. The term $g_{ij}(\bvu) = 1 / R_{ij}(\bvu)$ is the effective thermal conductance, with $R_{ij} = R_{i} + R_{j}$ and $R_{ij}(\bvu) = \ell/k(\bvu)A$, where $\ell$ is the distance between lumped masses, $k(\bvu)$ is the temperature-dependent thermal conductivity, and $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the interface. Similarly, the heat flux due to the Dirichlet boundary condition is given by $q_{iT} = g_{iT}\left(\bu_i - \bu_T\right)$. Now for component $i$, and noting that $\cN_i^b = \cN_i^u\cup\cN_i^w$, \cref{eqn_general_lcm} may be written as,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \bar{A}_i(\bu_i)\dot{\bu}_i &=\sum_{j\in\cN^u_i}\int_{e_{ij}}g_{ij}(\bvu)\left(\bu_i - \bu_j\right)de + \sum_{j\in\cN_i^w}\int_{e_{ij}}g_{ij}(\bvu)\left(\bu_i - \bw_j\right)de + \int_{e_{iq}}q_b de + \int_{e_{iT}}q_{T}de\\
        &= \sum_{j\in\cN_i^b\cup\left\{T\right\}}g_{ij}(\bvu)\left|e_{ij}\right|\bu_i - \sum_{j\in\cN_i^u}g_{ij}(\bvu)\left|e_{ij}\right|\bu_j - \sum_{j\in\cN_i^w}g_{ij}(\bvu)\left|e_{ij}\right|\bw_j + \left|e_{iq}\right|\bar{q}_b - g_{iT}(\bvu)\left|e_{iT}\right|\bar{T}_b\\
        &= \sum_{j\in\cN_i^{b}\cup\left\{T\right\}}\bar{B}_{ij}^{i}\bu_i + \sum_{j\in\cN_i^u}\bar{B}_{ij}^{j}\bu_j + \sum_{j\in\cN_i^w}\bar{B}_{ij}^{j}\bw_j + \bar{f}_i^{(b)}(t)
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
or, by leveraging heat flux continuity from \cref{eqn_interface_heat_flux_conservation},
\begin{align}
     \left(\int_{\Omega^{b}_i}\rho c_{p,i}(\bar{u}) d\Omega\right)\dot{\bu}_i &= \left(\sum_{j\in\cN^b_i}\int_{e_{ij}}g_{ij}\left(\bu_i - \bu_j\right)d\Gamma\right)\\
    &+ \sum_{j\in\cN_i^{c}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{ij}}g_{x,ij}\Delta\bw_j d\Gamma\right) + \int_{e_{iq}}q_b d\Gamma + \int_{e_{iT}}q_{T}d\Gamma
\end{align}

Collecting $N_B$ bulk and $N_{\Gamma}$ interface terms, the system of equations governing the coarse-grained temperature dynamics of the TPS with imperfect thermal contacts is written as the following coupled system of ODEs,
\begin{equation}
    \bvcA\left(\vs\right)\dot{\vs} = \bvcB\left(\vs\right) + \bvcF(t)\label{eqn_modified_lcm}
\end{equation}
where,
\begin{equation}
    \bvcA = \begin{bmatrix}
        \bar{\vA}(\bvu) & \vzero\\
        -\vD & \vI + \vG\odot\vS(\bvu)
    \end{bmatrix},\quad \bvcB = \begin{bmatrix}
        \bar{\vB}(\bvu) & \bar{\vK}(\bvu)\\
        \vzero & -\vG\odot\dot{\vS}(\bvu)
    \end{bmatrix},\quad \bvcF(t) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \bar{\vf}(t) \\ \vzero
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
and where $\vs = \left[\bvu,\Delta\bar{\vw}\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N_C + N_{\Gamma}}$, and $\bvcA,\bvcB\in\mathbb{R}^{(\NC + \NGamma)\times(\NC+\NGamma)}$ define the RPM. Note that the $\bvcA$ and $\bvcB$ matrices include temperature dependence that represents the primary source of non-linearities for the problems considered in this work.
