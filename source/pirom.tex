\section{Physics-Infused Reduced-Order Modeling}

Following our previous work in Ref.[VargasVenegas2026], the PIROM formulation starts by mathematically connecting the FOM to the RPM. 

The formulation of PIROM for TPS starts by mathematically connecting the FOM to the RPM. This connection is formally established through a coarse-graining procedure, which systematically reduces the spatial resolution of the DG-FEM to discover the lifted LCM model with contact temperature jump dynamics. Additionally, the coarse-graining procedure pinpoints residual terms in the RPM that facilitate the design of a data-driven memory closure to account for discarded spatial resolution.

\subsection{Deriving the Reduced-Physics Model via Coarse-Graining}

Following our previous work in Ref. [VargasVenegas2026], the LCM can be viewed as a zeroth-order (i.e., constant basis functions) DG-FEM with an extremely coarse mesh partition, and where the element-wise penalty factors become the conductance. The presence of imperfect interfaces motivates the modification of the coarse-graining procedure to explicitly introduce an additional dynamical system that is parametrized by TCRs, which is not present in the standard LCM formulation. The following discussion presents the derivation of the modified LCM dynamics via coarse-graining, and the PIROM formulation for residual dynamics.

\subsubsection{Coarse-Graining of States}

Consider a DG-FEM formulation as in \cref{eqn_dg_fem} with $M$ elements, $\NC$ components and $\NGamma$ interfaces; clearly, $M \gg \NC,\NGamma$. Let component $\Omega_i=\Omega_i^b\cup\Omega_i^c$ share a boundary with the imperfect interface $\Gamma_r$, i.e., $\partial\Omega_i^c\cap\Gamma_r\neq\emptyset$ with $\Omega_i^c\subset\Omega_i$, and define the sets of indices $\cV_i=\{j \:|\: E_j\subset\Omega_i^b\},\quad i=1,2,\dots,N_B$ and $\cF_i=\left\{j \:|\: \partial W_j\cap\Gamma_r\neq\emptyset,\: W_j\subset\Omega_i^c\right\},\quad i=1,2,\dots,N,\:r=1,2,\dots,N_C$ for elements in the bulk $\Omega_i^b$ and contact $\Omega_i^c$ sub-domains, respectively. Introduce the following states $\vw=\left[\vw^{-},\vw^{+}\right]^\top\in\mathbb{KP}$, where $\vw^{-}$ and $\vw^{+}$ are the states of elements on the negative and positive sub-domains conforming and interface, respectively. Note that $M=B+K$, and $\vw^{-}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{-}P}$ and $\vw^{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{+}P}$, with $Ks=K^{-}+K^{+}$ and where $K^{-}$ and $K^{+}$ denote the total number of elements on the negative and positive sides of imperfect interfaces, respectively.

Under such variable construction, the average temperatures on some bulk component $\Omega_i^b$ and interface $\Gamma_r$ are given by,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \bar{u}_i &= \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_i^{b}\right|}\sum_{m\in\cV_i}\int_{E_m} \vphi_m^\top\vu_m dE = \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_i^b\right|}\sum_{m\in\cV_i}\left|E_m\right|\vvarphi_m^{j\top}\vu_m, \quad i=1,\ldots,N_{C}\\
        \bar{w}_r^{-}(t) &= \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_r^{c,-}\right|}\sum_{m\in\cF_r^-}\int_{E_m} \vphi_m^\top\vw^{-}_m dE = \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_r^{c,-}\right|}\sum_{m\in\cF_r^{-}}\left|E_m\right|\vpsi_m^{r-}\vw^{-}_m, \quad r=1,2,\dots,N_{\Gamma}\\
        \bar{w}_r^{+}(t) &= \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_r^{c,+}\right|}\sum_{m\in\cF_r^+}\int_{E_m} \vphi_m^\top\vw^{+}_m dE = \frac{1}{\left|\Omega_r^{c,+}\right|}\sum_{m\in\cF_r^{+}}\left|E_m\right|\vpsi_m^{r+}\vw_m^{+},\quad r=1,2,\dots,\NGamma
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the orthogonal basis functions $\vvarphi_i^j,\vpsi_m^{j\pm}=\left[1,0,\dots,0\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$ if $i\in\cV_j$ and $m\in\cF_j^{\pm}$, respectively, and are zero otherwise. With the average temperatures $\left\{\bar{u}_i\right\}_{i=1}^{N_C}$, $\left\{\bar{w}_i^{-}\right\}_{i=1}^{\NGamma}$, and $\left\{\bar{w}_i^{+}\right\}_{i=1}^{N_{\Gamma}}$, the DG-FEM states of elements on domains $\Omega_i^b$, $\Omega_r^{c,-}$, and $\Omega_r^{c,+}$ are written as,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \vu_i &= \sum_{k=1}^{N_C}\vvarphi_i^k\bar{u}_k + \delta\vu_i,\quad i\in\cV_j,\quad j=1,2,\dots,B\label{eqn_bulk_element_states}\\
        \vw_{ir}^{-} &= \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\Gamma}}\vpsi_k^{r-}\bar{w}_k^{-} + \delta\vw_{ir}^{-},\quad i\in\cF_r^{-},\quad r=1,2,\dots,N_{\Gamma}\label{eqn_negative_element_states}\\
        \vw_{ir}^{+} &= \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\Gamma}}\vpsi_k^{r+}\bar{w}_k^{+} + \delta\vw_{ir}^{+},\quad i\in\cF_r^{+},\quad r=1,2,\dots,N_{\Gamma}\label{eqn_positive_element_states}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
\noindent where $\vvarphi_i^{k},\vpsi_i^{k\pm} = [1,0,\dots,0]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$ if $i\in\cV_k$ and $\cF_r^{\pm}$, respectively.

The \crefrange{eqn_bulk_element_states}{eqn_positive_element_states} are collected in matrix form,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \vu &= \vPhi\bvu + \delta\vu,\quad \bvu=\vPhi^{\dagger}\vu\\
        \vw &= \vPi\bar{\vw} + \delta\vw,\quad \bar{\vw}=\vPi^{\dagger}\vw
    \end{align}\label{eqn_state_factorization}
\end{subequations}
where $\vPhi\in\mathbb{R}^{BP\times N_B}$ is a matrix of $B\times N_{B}$ blocks, with the $(i,j)$-th block as $\vvarphi_i^j$, $\vPhi^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{C}\times BP}$ is the left inverse of $\vPhi$, with the $(i,j)$-th block as $\vvarphi_i^{j\dagger}=\frac{|E_i|}{|\Omega_j|}\vvarphi_i^{jT}$ and $\delta\vu$ is the collection of deviations from the average temperature. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
    \vPi = \begin{bmatrix}
        \vM & \vzero\\
        \vzero & \vN
    \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{KP\times N_C},\quad \vPi^{\dagger} = \begin{bmatrix}
        \vM^{\dagger} & \vzero\\
        \vzero & \vN^{\dagger}
    \end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\NGamma\times KP}
\end{equation}
where $\vM\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{-}P\times\NGamma}$ and $\vN\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{+}P\times\NGamma}$ correspond to matrices that project the average interface temperatures $\bvw=\left[\bvw^{-},\bvw^{+}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{2\NGamma}$ to the full-order interface DG-FEM state. Therefore, $\vPi\in\mathbb{R}^{KP\times 2\NGamma}$ is a matrix of $K\times 2\NGamma$ blocks with the $(i,j)$-th block as $\vpsi_i^j$, $\vPi^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\NGamma\times KP}$ is the left inverse of $\vPi$ with the $(i,j)$-th block as $\vpsi_i^{j\dagger} = \frac{\left|E_i\right|}{\left|\Omega_j^c\right|}\vpsi_i^{j\top}$ and $\delta\vw$ is the collection of deviations from the interfacial average temperatures. By definition, the matrices defined above satisfy,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align*}
        \vPhi^{\dagger}\vPhi &= \vI, & \vPhi^{\dagger}\delta\vu  &= \vzero\\
        \vPi^{\dagger}\vPi &= \vI, & \vPi^{\dagger}\delta\vw &= \vzero
    \end{align*}
\end{subequations}
For brevity, the states in \cref{eqn_state_factorization} are collectively written as,
\begin{equation}
    \vx = \vX\bvx + \delta\vx,\quad \bvx = \vX^{\dagger}\vx
\end{equation}
where $\vx=\left[\vu,\vw\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{MP}$, $\vX\in\mathbb{R}^{MP\times (N_B+N_C)}$, and $\vX^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{(N_B+N_C)\times MP}$.

\subsubsection{Coarse-Graining of Dynamics}

Next, the coarse-graining procedure follows our previous work in Ref. [VargasVenegas2026], where the higher-order temperature modes are neglected in the DG-FEM dynamics, leading to a spatially averaged transient modeling of the TPS temperature dynamics. The presence of imperfect thermal contacts introduces additional modeling considerations to account for temperature discontinuities across TPS layers. The following discussion summarizes the critical results from coarse-graining, while detailed derivations are provided in Appendix [X].

Consider a function of the states in the form $\vM\left(\vx\right)\vg(\vx)$ with $\vx=\left[\vu,\vw\right]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{MP}$ and $M=B+K$, where $\vg(\vx):\mathbb{R}^{MP}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{MP}$ is a vector-valued function, and $\vM(\vx):\mathbb{R}^{MP}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p\times MP}$ is a matrix-valued function with an arbitrary dimension $p$. Define the projection matrices $\vP = \vX\vX^{\dagger}$ where,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \vX &= \text{diag}\left(\vPhi,\vPi\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{MP\times (N_{B} + N_C)}\\
        \vX^{\dagger} &= \text{diag}\left(\vPhi^{\dagger},\vPi^{\dagger}\right) \in\mathbb{R}^{(N_B + N_C)\times MP}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
and the projection operator,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \cP\left[\vM(\vx)\vg(\vx)\right] &= \vM(\vP\vx)\vP\vg(\vP\vx)\\
        &= \vM\left(\vX\vX^{\dagger}\vx\right)\vX\vX^{\dagger}\vg\left(\vX\vX^{\dagger}\right)\\
        &= \vM\left(\vX\bvx\right)\vX\vX^{\dagger}\vg\left(\vX\bvx\right)
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
so that the resulting function depends only on the resolved variables $\bvx=\left[\bar{\vu},\bvw^{-},\bvw^{+}\right]^{N_{B}+N_C}$; note that an averaged temperature difference $\Delta\bvw = \bvw^- - \bvw^+$ may be computed between two contact sub-domains conforming an imperfect interface, which becomes useful for subsequent TCR identifications. Correspondingly, the residual operator is $\cQ = \cI - \cP$ so that $\cQ\left[\vM(\vx)\vg(\vx)\right] = \vM(\vx)\vg(\vx) - \cP\left[\vM(\vx)\vg(\vx)\right]$. When the function is not separable, the projection operator is simply given by $\cP\left[\vg(\vx)\right]=\vg\left(\vX\bvx\right)$.

Subsequently, the operators defined above are applied to coarse-grain the dynamics. First, the DG-FEM model in \cref{eqn_dg_fem} is written as,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\vx} = \vcA^{-1}\left[\vcB(\vx)\vx + \vcF(t)\right] = \vr(\vx,t)
\end{equation}
Multiplying both sides by $\vX^{\dagger}$ produces,
\begin{equation}
    \vX^{\dagger}\dot{\vx} = \vX^{\dagger}\left(\vX\dot{\bvx} + \delta\dot{\vx}\right) = \dot{\bvx} = \vX^{\dagger}\vr(\vx,t)
\end{equation}
and applying the projection and residual operator yields,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \dot{\bvx} &= \cP\left[\vX^{\dagger}\vr(\vx,t)\right] + \cQ\left[\vX^{\dagger}\vr(\vx,t)\right]\\
        &= \vr^{(1)}(\bvx,t) + \vr^{(2)}(\vx,t)
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\vr^{(1)}(\bvx,t)$ is the resolved dynamics, which depends only on $\bvx$, and $\vr^{(2)}(\vx,t)$ is the residual dynamics, which depends on the full-order state $\vx$. Detailed derivations of the resolved $\vr^{(1)}$ and residual $\vr^{(2)}$ dynamics are provided in Appendix \hl{[X]}, and the key results are stated below.

First, the resolved dynamics becomes exactly the LCM, with the temperature jump dynamics formulated as a \emph{zero-energy storage} assumption on the contact sub-domain component of the DG-FEM dynamics. Specifically, the coarse-grained mass matrices are given by,
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        \bvA &= \vW_u\left(\vPhi^{\dagger}\vA\left(\vPhi\bvu\right)^{-1}\vPhi\right)^{-1}\\
        \bvD &= \vW_w\left(\vPi^{\dagger}\vD\left(\vPi\bvw\right)^{-1}\vPi\right)^{-1}
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\vW_u\in\mathbb{R}^{N_B\times N_B}$ and $\vW_w\in\mathbb{R}^{N_C\times N_C}$ are diagonal weight matrices such that $\left[\vW_u\right]_{i} = \left|\cV_k\right|$ if $i\in\cV_k$, and $\left[\vW_c\right]_i=\left|\cF_k\right|$ if $i\in\cF_k$. Similarly, the coarse-grained stiffness matrices are,

        \bvF &= \vW_w\vPi^{\dagger}\vF\left(\vPhi\bvu\right)\vPi
